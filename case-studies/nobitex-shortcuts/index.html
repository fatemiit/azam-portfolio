The signal

A support queue full of “where is it?”

Our goal sounded operational: lower support costs and make users happier. The real work started when we sat with the support agents and asked what people were actually calling about.

Many calls weren’t about missing money or failed trades. They were about finding things: where to invite a friend, how to deposit, where the price alert went. People were calling us to get around the app.

How we found the problem

Three steps, from anecdotes to evidence.

Step 1

Talk to support

Agents told us a big part of their calls came from people who couldn’t find a feature.

Step 2

List the topics

We turned those conversations into a list of the most frequent call topics.

Step 3

Check the paths

We validated the list against real user paths in Google Analytics and other tools.

Conclusion

People couldn’t find certain features, so they called support to ask where they were.

People understood crypto fine. They just couldn’t find things in our app.

The root cause

Two taps too deep

The paths showed a pattern. Some of the most important features were two or three steps deep, sometimes more. To deposit, you had to open the wallet first. To invite a friend, you had to dig through the menu. Every extra step was a chance to get lost.

The deeper issue was information architecture. In places, the app’s structure was complicated, and it didn’t match what people already expected from the other financial apps they used every day.

Options

Four ways to fix it

We listed every solution we could think of and compared them on two things: how much each would help, and how fast we could ship it while support was overloaded.

Option 1

Rework the information architecture

Upside: fixes access to every feature. Downside: months of work while support stays overloaded.

Option 2 · Chosen

A shortcut row on the dashboard

Upside: one-tap access to the most-asked features. Downside: limited space, so not everything fits.

Option 3

A better FAQ

Upside: fewer calls if people read it. Downside: most people don’t read FAQs.

Option 4

A bottom sheet before contacting support

Upside: answers navigation questions before the call. Downside: too many flows to explain in a sheet.

What option 4 looked like

The bottom sheet we explored. A good idea, but it didn’t scale to every flow in the app.

Contact support anyway

Reworking the whole architecture was the better long-term answer, but it would take too long. The shortcut gave us most of the value right away, so we shipped it first and kept working on the architecture in later releases.

The shipped shortcut row

Smart defaults

The features behind the most support calls (deposit, withdraw, open orders and price alerts) are in the shortcut by default.

Editable

Anyone can swap the defaults for the features they use most.

Card sorting

In the edit screen, features are grouped into categories from a card-sorting study, so people find them the way they think about them.

We put the features with the most support calls into the shortcut by default, on the dashboard where everyone sees them. Then we let people edit the row themselves.

Looking back

What this project taught me

Lesson

Support is a research tool

The fastest route to real problems was sitting next to the people who answer the phone.

Lesson

Ship the 80% first

A focused fix today can beat the perfect overhaul in six months.

Lesson

Defaults + control

Smart defaults help most people; editing keeps power users happy.
